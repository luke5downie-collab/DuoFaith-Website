Lent 2027

A Lent challenge for couples: ten ideas for 40 days, and the shared fast where both partners' apps stay locked until you have both read

Quick answer

The strongest Lent challenge for couples is a shared social media fast: for 40 days, neither of you opens the apps that steal your mornings until you have both read Scripture. Below are ten ideas, plus how DuoFaith Couples Mode keeps both phones locked each morning until you have both finished.

A Lent challenge for couples does not need to be complicated. It needs one thing you both give up, one thing you both take up, and a way to keep each other honest for 40 days.

Ash Wednesday is 10 February 2027 and Easter Sunday is 28 March 2027. Below are ten ideas, from a shared social media fast to a nightly gratitude exchange. The first is the hardest to fake, so that is the one we walk you through in DuoFaith.

Idea 1: a shared social media fast, Scripture before the phone

The first and strongest idea is simple. For 40 days, neither of you opens social media until you have read Scripture.

You can do a social media fast for couples on trust alone. Agree on the apps and a passage, and check in each evening. The trouble is that the phone is in your hand at 6:30am and your spouse is still asleep.

DuoFaith removes the argument. You each pick the apps that pull at you. At your lock time each morning they shield themselves, and they stay shielded until you finish a devotional of about two minutes: a passage, a short lesson, one reflection question, and a written prayer. In Couples Mode, both phones lock, and neither unlocks until you have both finished. You can see whether your spouse has read yet, and send one nudge a day.

It is the sequence from Bible before phone: put the Word in front of the scroll, and decide it the night before so 7am is not a fight. For Lent, you do it together.

Ideas 2 to 10: more Lent ideas for married couples

Pick two or three of these to go with the fast. Nine at once is a recipe for quitting by week two.

  • 2. Pray out loud together once a day. One minute before bed counts.
  • 3. Read one Gospel together. Mark has 16 chapters, which spread easily over 40 days.
  • 4. Give up one shared comfort. Takeout, streaming, or dessert. Give the money saved to a cause you pick together.
  • 5. A nightly phone curfew. Both phones charge in the kitchen from 9pm.
  • 6. One act of service a week. Six weeks, six people outside your home.
  • 7. A note every Sunday. A short letter to each other. Six each by Easter.
  • 8. Fast one meal a week together. Use the time to pray for each other.
  • 9. A gratitude exchange. Each night, name one thing you are thankful for about the other person.
  • 10. Confess and forgive on Fridays. Name one thing you got wrong, ask for forgiveness, and let it go.

None of these need an app. The fast is where an app earns its place, because it is the one your habits will fight hardest.

How to hold each other to it for 40 days

Lent is long. Enthusiasm carries you through Ash Wednesday. Something sturdier has to carry you through week four.

Two are better than one, because they have a good reward for their labor. (Ecclesiastes 4:9, WEB)
  • Decide once. Write down what you are giving up and taking up, and put it on the fridge.
  • Make it mutual. A challenge where one partner polices the other turns sour fast. Both of you carry the same stake.
  • Make it visible. A streak you can both see does more than a promise you both remember. In DuoFaith the streak is shared, so if one of you skips, it breaks for both. That is the point.
  • Agree what happens after a slip. Grace, then start again the next morning.

Doing a Lenten phone fast alone? Three honest ways to keep social media off

Maybe your spouse is not joining in. Here are three ways to do a Lenten phone fast challenge without DuoFaith, and the gap in each.

  • Downtime with a passcode your spouse holds. In Settings, open Screen Time, turn on Downtime or an App Limit for the apps you are giving up, then set a Screen Time passcode and let your spouse choose it. Without the code you cannot tap Ignore Limit. It is free and built in. The gap: it only works if the other person never hands over the code, and Downtime runs on a schedule, so the apps come back at the set hour whether you have prayed or not. Apple explains it in its Screen Time guide.
  • Delete the apps. Blunt, and it works. The gap: reinstalling takes 30 seconds, and the browser still gets you there.
  • A strict blocker. Several apps are built for this. Some pair with a physical tag you have to tap, some add stricter session modes, and some put a pause in front of every app you open. Good tools. None is tied to Scripture.

Then there is the swap. Instead of removing the apps for 40 days, you open them only after Scripture. That is what DuoFaith does, solo too. See how to read the Bible instead of scrolling.

How to set up the shared fast in DuoFaith before Ash Wednesday

  1. Download DuoFaith on both iPhones

    Get it from the App Store a few days before 10 February so the first morning is not a setup morning. No account needed.

  2. Start the free trial of Pro

    Couples Mode is part of Pro. Each of you starts the free trial during onboarding, which is what gets you into the app.

  3. Each pick the apps you are fasting from

    Choose the apps that steal your mornings. Only those are locked. Calls, texts and maps stay open.

  4. Set your lock time

    The default is 8:00 AM. You can each set your own. From that hour the apps stay shielded until the devotional is done.

  5. Pair with your spouse

    One of you creates an invite code in Couples Mode and the other enters it. The code expires, so do it while you are together.

  6. Read, finish and unlock, every morning through Easter

    You each read the same day's devotional on your own phone and tap Finish and Unlock. Apps release once you have both finished and you open DuoFaith. Keep the shared streak going to Easter.

Common questions

Is there a Lent challenge couples can do together on their phones?

Yes. A shared social media fast is the simplest one. For 40 days, neither of you opens the apps you have picked until you have both read Scripture. DuoFaith Couples Mode does the locking: both phones shield your chosen apps each morning and neither unlocks until you have both finished. Couples Mode is part of Pro, which starts with a free trial.

Does DuoFaith have a special Lent devotional series?

No. DuoFaith serves one new devotional every calendar day from its library, and it is a general devotional, not a Lent series. If you want Lent-specific reading, pair the DuoFaith morning lock with idea 3 and read a Gospel together in the evening.

Do we have to keep the lock on Sundays during Lent?

DuoFaith locks every day, Sundays included, and the shared streak resets if either of you skips a day. Many Christians treat the Sundays of Lent as feast days. Since the devotional takes about two minutes, you can keep reading each Sunday and still enjoy the day with your apps open.

What if only one of us has an iPhone?

DuoFaith is iPhone only, so Couples Mode needs two iPhones. If one of you has an Android phone, the iPhone partner can still run the solo lock in DuoFaith, and the other can use one of the three no-app methods above.
