Is there a screen time app for couples that holds both of you to it?

Most apps for two people and their phones work in one of two ways.

Monitoring apps. One person can see the other person's usage, app by app. These are built for parents and children. Between two adults, they turn one of you into the supervisor, and that can quietly damage trust.

Shared timer apps. You both agree to stay off your phones for an hour and try not to break it. Good for a date night. They rely on willpower in the moment, which is the exact thing you were short on.

Apple's built-in Screen Time can help a little. You can set App Limits and let your partner hold the passcode. But that makes your partner the gatekeeper, and they can always type it in when you ask nicely.

What most couples want is simpler: a shared rule neither of you can bend, agreed once, so nobody is the bad guy at 7am.

That is what Couples Mode in DuoFaith does. It is not a monitoring app and cannot see your partner's usage. It locks the apps you each picked until you have both read.

How Couples Mode works: two phones, one lock

Couples Mode links two iPhones with an invite code. Here is a normal morning once you are paired.

  • At your lock time, the apps you chose lock on your phone. Your partner's chosen apps lock on theirs, at the time they set.
  • You each open DuoFaith and read the same day's devotional on your own phone. About two minutes.
  • Finishing first does not unlock your phone. You see your partner's status: waiting, or done.
  • If they have not read yet, you can send one nudge a day. A gentle notification, not a lecture.
  • Once you have both finished and you open DuoFaith, your apps release.

You share one streak. If one of you skips, it breaks for both. That is a little harsh, and it is also why it works. You are both protecting the same thing.

Each of you picks your own apps. One might lock Instagram and TikTok, the other YouTube and a news app. Calls, texts and maps keep working.

You can unpair any time and go back to solo. If you are apart for a while, the long distance couples guide covers how the same setup works across a distance.

What you actually read each morning

A couples screen time app is only as good as its unlock. If the unlock is a chore, you will both resent it by day four.

In DuoFaith, the unlock is a short daily devotional. Every calendar day there is a new one, and both of you get the same passage. It has a Bible passage, a little context, a plain-language lesson, a takeaway, one reflection question, and a written prayer.

The reflection step is one question and a short pause of about 15 seconds. There is nothing to type and nothing is saved. You sit with it for a moment, then move on.

The devotional is general Christian teaching, not marriage-specific. Some couples talk about the question over coffee afterward. Both ways are fine.

The Bible text is the World English Bible. It reads plainly:

Two are better than one, because they have a good reward for their labor. (Ecclesiastes 4:9)

Not just couples: an app blocker with an accountability partner

Couples Mode is named for couples, but nothing in it checks your relationship status. It works for any two people who want to keep each other honest:

  • A husband and wife, or an engaged or dating couple.
  • Two friends from church who both want mornings in the Word.
  • A parent and an adult child.
  • Roommates who keep losing the first hour of the day to their phones.

If you have been searching for a screen time accountability partner app, this is the same idea with a Christian spine. Your partner sees one thing: whether you have read today. That is a lighter kind of accountability, and for most people it is enough.

You each pick your own apps and lock time, so it does not matter if one of you is a 5am runner and the other starts at 8. The earlier riser waits until the other has read.

How to set up a couples screen time lock in DuoFaith

  1. Download DuoFaith on both iPhones

    Get it from the App Store on your phone and your partner's phone. Each of you starts a free trial of Pro, which is what unlocks Couples Mode.

  2. Each pick the apps that steal your mornings

    Choose the apps you want locked. You do not have to match. The apps you pick get shielded and nothing else. Calls, texts and maps are never touched.

  3. Each set your own lock time

    The default is 8:00 AM. Pick the hour that fits your morning. Your partner picks theirs. The lock starts then and stays until the devotional is done.

  4. Pair with an invite code

    One of you creates an invite code in Couples Mode. The other enters it. The code expires, so do it while you are together or on a call.

  5. Read the day's devotional, each on your own phone

    Passage, lesson, one reflection question, a prayer. About two minutes. You can see whether your partner has read yet, and send one nudge if not.

  6. Finish, then unlock together

    Once you have both finished and you open DuoFaith, your apps release. Keep the shared streak going one morning at a time.

Common questions

Does DuoFaith track screen time or show usage reports?

No. DuoFaith does not track your screen time and does not show usage reports. It locks the apps you pick each morning until you finish the devotional, and that is all. If you want charts of how long you spent in each app, Apple's built-in Screen Time already shows that for free in Settings.

Can I see what my partner is doing on their phone?

No. DuoFaith is not a monitoring app and cannot see your partner's usage. The one thing you can see is their status for today: waiting, or done. You can send one nudge a day. That is the whole picture, and it is on purpose. It keeps the focus on the habit, not on checking up on each other.

Will it block calls, texts or maps?

No. The apps you choose are the ones that lock, and nothing else. Calls, messages, maps and anything you did not pick keep working as normal. Each partner picks their own list, so what is locked on your phone can be different from what is locked on theirs.

What if we are in different time zones?

It still works. Each partner sets their own lock time in their own time zone. The earlier riser reads first and then waits until the partner has read. Apps release on each phone once you have both finished and you open DuoFaith. Both of you read the same day's devotional, so you are still in the same passage.
