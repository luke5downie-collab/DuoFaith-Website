Couples guide

A devotional app for long distance couples: the same passage each day, and neither partner's apps unlock until you have both read

Quick answer

Living apart makes shared faith habits hard. Mornings start at different times, and "we should read together" turns into "we meant to".

A long distance couples devotional app can close some of that gap. Before you download anything, here is exactly what DuoFaith does and does not do.

Both of you read the same daily devotional, each on your own iPhone. It is a general devotional, not marriage-specific content. It takes about two minutes: a passage, a short lesson, one reflection question with a short pause, and a prayer. Nothing to type, no shared notes. What makes it a couples tool is Couples Mode. You can see whether your partner has read today, and neither of your locked apps opens until you have both finished. It is iPhone only, and Couples Mode is part of Pro, with a free trial.

How couples who live apart keep a daily devotional going (with or without an app)

Start with the honest answer: you do not need an app to do this.

  • Pick the same passage. Agree on a book of the Bible and read one chapter a day, each on your own.
  • Send one line. After you read, text your partner a single sentence about what stood out.
  • Use a shared plan. YouVersion lets you start a reading plan with a friend and see each other's progress.
  • Book a weekly call. Once a week, talk through what you read over video.

The gap is the morning itself. None of these stop you from opening Instagram first and forgetting. When you live in the same house, your partner sees you skip. When you live apart, nobody does.

That is the problem DuoFaith is built for. It does not replace the call. It makes sure the reading happens, every morning, on both phones. For the solo version of that idea, see Bible before phone.

What a long distance couples devotional app needs to do

Four things matter more than a long feature list.

  1. Same content, same day. You should both be reading the same passage, so a quick text about it makes sense.
  2. You can see each other. Not a chat. Just a simple signal: has my partner read yet today?
  3. Something at stake. A gentle reason to do it before the day runs away.
  4. Short. A long study dies in week two. Two minutes survives a busy Tuesday.

DuoFaith is built around those four. Each day there is one devotional for everyone: a Bible passage, a little context, a plain-language lesson, a takeaway, one reflection question, and a written prayer. The reflection step gives you about 15 seconds to sit with the question. Nothing to type.

Couples Mode adds the visibility and the stakes. You see your partner's status as waiting, done, or both done, and the apps you chose stay locked until you have both finished. For more on that idea, read our guide to a screen time app for couples.

How Couples Mode works when you are in two different cities

Couples Mode links two iPhones with an invite code.

Here is a normal morning:

  • At your lock time, the apps you picked go behind a shield. Your partner's do the same at theirs. You each pick your own apps and your own hour.
  • You open DuoFaith and read the day's devotional. About two minutes.
  • Finishing first does not unlock your phone. Your status changes to done, and your partner can see it.
  • If they have not read yet, you can send one nudge a day.
  • Your apps unlock once you have both finished and you open DuoFaith.

You also share a streak. If one of you skips a day, it resets for both. Nobody wants to be the one who broke the streak from 800 miles away.

There is no unlocking early because you had a busy morning. Deleting the app removes the lock, so it is a commitment, not a cage. You can unpair any time and go back to solo.

Bible study apps for long distance couples: where DuoFaith fits

Be clear about one thing: DuoFaith is not a Bible study app. If you want to go deep together, use a tool built for that.

  • A shared YouVersion plan. Pick a plan, invite your partner, and read it together at your own pace.
  • A study guide over a video call. Buy the same printed guide, work through a chapter a week, and talk it out on Sunday night.

Those give you depth and conversation. What they do not give you is a reason to open them at 7am instead of TikTok. Most couples who quit a shared study never decided to quit. They just kept meaning to catch up.

That is where DuoFaith sits: underneath the study, as the daily habit layer. Two minutes in the Word before your apps open, on both phones. Do that first, then go deep in your study tool when you have time. The habit protects the study.

What to know before you download (the honest limits)

A few things people ask about, answered straight:

  • The devotional is general, not marriage-specific. It is Scripture, a lesson, a question and a prayer. It is not about your relationship.
  • There are no shared notes or chat. You see your partner's status and can send one nudge a day. The conversation happens on your usual call or text thread.
  • It is iPhone only. Both of you need an iPhone. It uses Apple's Screen Time controls to shield apps.
  • It only locks what you pick. Calls, texts, maps and anything you did not choose stay open.
  • No account. No email, no password. Couples sync uses an anonymous session, and Screen Time data never leaves your phone.
  • Pricing. DuoFaith is free to download, and every new user starts with a free trial of Pro. Couples Mode needs Pro, at $9.99 a month or $49.99 a year.

How to set up a shared devotional across two phones

  1. Download DuoFaith on both iPhones

    Each of you gets it from the App Store. There is no account to make and no email to enter.

  2. Start the free trial of Pro

    Every new user starts with the trial. Couples Mode is part of Pro, so you both go through this step.

  3. Pick the apps that steal your mornings

    Each partner chooses their own list. Only what you pick gets locked. Calls and texts stay open.

  4. Set your own lock time

    The default is 8:00 AM. In different time zones? Each of you sets the hour that fits your own morning.

  5. Pair with an invite code

    One of you creates a code in Couples Mode, and the other enters it. Codes expire, so do it while you are both on a call.

  6. Read, finish, and check on each other

    Each morning you both read the same devotional. Tap Finish &amp; Unlock when you are done. Your apps unlock once you have both finished and you open DuoFaith.

Common questions

Does DuoFaith work for couples in different time zones?

Yes. Each partner sets their own lock time, so your apps lock in your morning and your partner's lock in theirs. One thing to plan for: your apps stay locked until you have both finished and you open DuoFaith. If your partner is several hours behind you, the one who is ahead waits longer. Some couples handle that by having the person behind read before bed.

Do we both read the same devotional?

Yes. You each open DuoFaith on your own iPhone and read the day's devotional. It is the same passage, lesson, reflection question and prayer for both of you. It is a general devotional, not marriage-specific content, and there are no shared notes inside the app. Most couples talk about it on their usual call or text thread.

What happens if my partner forgets to read?

Your apps stay locked until they finish and you open DuoFaith. You can send one nudge a day, which lands as a notification on their phone. If a full day is missed, the shared streak resets for both of you. Deleting the app removes the lock entirely.

Is Couples Mode free?

DuoFaith is free to download, and every new user starts with a free trial of Pro. Couples Mode is part of Pro, which costs $9.99 a month or $49.99 a year. Couples Mode stops and the pair is unlinked.
